How to use it

  1. Choose a photo, or open the 3D tab and choose a .glb file. You can also drop it on the picture.
  2. Pick a resolution and a colour setting. The preview updates as you move the sliders. For photos, drag the line to compare with the original.
  3. Press Download PNG. The Size menu gives the exact low-resolution pixels, or the same picture enlarged x2 or x4 without smoothing, which stays sharp when you post it.

Dither is the checkered texture that hides colour banding, soft blur and scanlines imitate a TV screen, and fog and vignette darken or haze the picture. All of them can stay at zero.

Questions

What is the PS1 look?

It is the picture of a 1990s 32-bit console game: low resolution (around 320 x 240), few colours with visible dither, blocky nearest-filtered textures, fog and a soft CRT image. This tool recreates the resolution, colour depth, dither, scanlines and fog. It is an approximation, not an emulator.

Does it warp or wobble the picture like old hardware did?

No, on purpose. Textures are drawn straight and nothing in the 3D view snaps or jitters, so the result stays clean and readable.

What is dithering?

Dithering mixes pixels of two nearby colours in a fixed pattern, so a smooth gradient still looks smooth with few colours instead of showing hard bands. This tool uses a 4 x 4 Bayer pattern, a classic ordered dither. The slider sets how strong it is.

What is 15-bit colour?

5 bits per channel: 32 levels of red, 32 of green and 32 of blue, 32,768 colours in total. The 64 and 16 colour presets pick a palette that fits your image.

Is it free?

Yes. No account, no limit. The small credit in the corner is on by default and you can switch it off.

Are my images uploaded?

No. Photos and 3D models are processed by JavaScript inside your browser tab and never leave your device. The page has no analytics and sets no cookies. The only files it loads are its own, and the 3D viewer code when you open the 3D tab.

Which files work?

Photos: whatever your browser can open (JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF). 3D: a single binary glTF file (.glb); files compressed with Draco, meshopt or KTX2 are not supported. Textures are drawn without smoothing. Large photos are shrunk in your browser, which can take a second.
